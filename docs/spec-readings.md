# Spec readings

Places where SPEC.md leaves room for more than one reading, the reading the
code takes, and why. Each was checked against the spec text. One reading
contradicted the spec and was changed (marked below); the rest stand.

| Section | Reading | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| §5.1 odds gate | "The known enemy mobile ground army metal" leaves out enemy commanders. | Our side of the ratio counts combat units only (the spec leaves our commander out). Counting theirs would make the two sides unequal, and a commander's metal (2500 in the sim) would keep the gate shut for most of the early game. |
| §5.7 commander fight or flee | "The threat metal" is the metal of the commander's danger contacts: seen, standing, armed, non-air, non-frame contacts within `cmd.danger_radius` (or the weapon range of a contact on water). | The fight test belongs to the commander-danger rule, and those contacts are what the commander then fights, d-guns or flees from. The exposure sum earlier in §5.7 also counts contacts not seen for up to 150 ticks and long-range units well outside 450, which would make the commander flee from threats that aren't there. |
| §5.3 counter lean | A role triggers when its count's share of the *whole* histogram is at least `mix.counter_trigger`. | This is what the text says ("its share of the histogram"). The histogram includes static defence, which is one of the roles that triggers a lean, so leaving buildings out of the total would not make sense. |
| §5.7 chase leash | The 675 leash (1.5 × `cmd.danger_radius`) is measured from the commander to its target. | It is a radius round the commander, like the danger radius it is scaled from. The spec gives no other anchor, such as a start point or the frame. |
| §4.3 rule 0, test only | The economy test lets energy stay stalled for at most 30 s in a row. | The spec sets no tolerance. Rule 0 makes a solar the next job, but the builder may be one job (and a walk) away, and the solar takes time to build. 30 s covers that reaction and still catches a missing answer. It is a test bound, not an AI behaviour. |
| §5.8 air patrol | The "base edge" is the distance from home to the furthest own building. | The spec uses the term without defining it. §4.6 uses "outermost own building's distance" for the same idea, and the clamp to 200..900 covers tiny and sprawling bases. |
| §5.9 ferry landing stall | A stalled loaded sea hull puts its landing point's `ferry.refusal_cell` cell in the refusal memory for `ferry.refusal_s`, then searches again from where it is. | This follows the spec text. The refusal memory is the same one the objective search reads (§5.2), so the army also avoids that landing. |
| §5.12, §5.5 navy "same water" (changed) | "Same water" is the station's water body, the body of the fleet's station. Objectives, submarine hunts and the commander guard use it. Targets must also pass the engine's can-hit test. | The commander guard used to check only distance and can-hit. This contradicted "on the same water": a hull in a pond behind the commander was sent after a ship it could not reach. Now only hulls on the station's body guard (Fix: Guard the commander only from our water). |
| §4.2 saving (new) | Saving starts whether or not a site exists. It ends when the saved-for item is affordable but has no site. | The spec says saving does not depend on a site, and that only economy types may be taken "until the saved-for item is built". If the item has no site it is never built, so the saving would never end. A land mirror showed this: metal full, every builder idle for ten minutes. Ending the saving only once the item is affordable keeps "saving does not depend on a site" for the whole saving window. |
| §4.2 rules 8 and 11, §5.7 commander reclaim (new) | A builder reclaims only features reachable on its own layer. The commander also reclaims only on ground the land builders reach from home, and only where it is not exposed (§5.7). | §4.2 says a site must be on the builder's side of reachability; reclaim targets are read the same way. The commander plans on its own layer, and in the sim that layer is amphibious, so both commanders waded the strait to the same wreck and fought over it in deep water. The exposure test is the one §5.7 already uses for builders and for the commander's repairers. |
| §4.11 commander repair (new) | An "eligible builder" must be able to get to the commander: a flyer always, a ship on naval-reachable water, a ground builder only when the commander stands on ground that home's ground layer reaches. | A constructor sent to a commander in deep water walks to the shore and its repair is rejected; the dispatch then sends the next one. Structures already skip unreachable ones (§4.11), and the same test applies to the commander. |
| §5.9 sea lift (sim, new) | The sim's shipyards now build a sea transport (ARMTSHIP / CORTSHIP, capacity 8). | The spec's answer to a water map is the sea lift: the shipyard queues the sea transport when the ferry is raised or overdue, and a sea lift is preferred over an air lift. The sim had no sea transport, so the AI fell back on one-unit air trips that were shot down, and the armies waited on their shores until the time limit. The spec gives no rule for giving up on a ferry; with the sea lift none was needed in these games. |
| §4.2 saving for the first air plant (new) | The saving test for the air plant leaves out the metal the factories commit (frames inside a factory, assistants included). While the AI saves for it, factories queue only builders, as under the tier-two reserve (§4.4). A saving that outlasts its window unbuilt ends, and that item is not saved for again for one more window. An affordable item whose site is refused (contested, failed) also ends the saving. | §4.2 names the first air plant for the long `eco.save_tech_s` window, but with every unit a factory builds counted as committed spend, net income was at or below zero, so the window test never passed and the plant came after 13 minutes or never. Holding all saving (shipyards too) made the water mirrors worse, so the hold is kept to the item the spec singles out. The window cap keeps a site that never frees from holding the factories forever. |
| §2.3 orders to a unit that cannot walk there (new) | An idle walking unit standing off our ground (more than one 32-step from any reachable point, as after a ferry landing) gets no Move or AttackMove to a point on our ground. | §2.3 drops refused orders and regenerates them from fresh state, so an order that ends at once is staged again every pass. One stranded raider drew about 100 rally moves a minute, each taking a token from the army's other units. The spec is silent on stranded units; skipping the order is the smallest change. |
| §5.9 rebuilding a lost sea lift (new) | Once the ferry is overdue (§5.9 urgency past `ferry.first_by_s`) and we own no shipyard although the faction has a sea transport, the shipyard is saved for like the first air plant (its test leaves out the factory spend and the factories queue only builders). Air transports do not count as owning the ferry here. | §5.9 prefers a sea lift and treats only a completed sea transport as owning a ferry where a shipyard exists; with the shipyard lost the AI fell back to one-unit air ferries that were shot down and the games timed out. The spec says nothing on when to stop ferrying by air; rebuilding the sea lift is the smallest change that follows its preference. |
| §4.6 contested sites afloat (new) | For a floating structure, armed contacts on dry ground our builders cannot walk to do not contest the site. Ships, and anything on our ground, still do; their guns are still the under-guns test's. | On the water map every shipyard site lies mid-strait, within 450 of the enemy army on the far shore, so a lost shipyard could never be rebuilt. Those units cannot reach the site across water; §4.6 already refuses sites in their weapon reach separately. |
